A physical mixnet · draft

Your parcel, carried by mules who have no idea where it's going.

MuleNet sends a box through a chain of friends' hubs. Each hub peels one layer, re-boxes it and passes it on, knowing only who handed it over and who's next. Nobody along the way, not the hubs, not the post, can connect the sender to the recipient.

  • The software exists and passes its tests: the onion labels, the hub directory, the receipts. It ran end to end on the Logos test network and in Basecamp.
  • No real box has gone through a real hub. The first trial will be a 3D-printed mule passed between a few friends.

We're publishing early so the rough edges get found before people start trying it for real.

The trick

Onion routing, but with hooves.

Mixnets hide who's talking to whom by passing messages through relays that each strip one layer of encryption. MuleNet does the same thing with cardboard.

The onion
A label that peels

The QR label is a fixed-size onion. Each hub scans it and learns only where to send the box next, then prints a fresh label for the next leg. Every label is the same size, so no hub can tell how far the box has come.

The layers
One sleeve per mule

The item sits inside one sealed sleeve per hop. Each hub takes off exactly one and checks its seal code. The hubs on either side of an honest hub never even see the same wrapping.

The disguise
New box, same weight

Hubs throw the old box away, use a plain standard one and pad it to a standard weight. They hold the box a few random days and ship on fixed batch days, so arrival and departure don't line up.

The receipts
Hee-haws over Logos

Each hub posts "received" and "shipped" receipts over Logos Messaging. They're sealed so only the sender, and the hubs either side, can read them. The sender sees progress; nobody else sees a route.

    Mule welfare
    Every mule has a donkey for a father. We take family seriously: nobody here gets made an ass of.

    The hubs are the mules. They do the carrying, so they get the protections. (A donkey dad is called a jack. A jackass is anyone who hands a mule something it can't see into and hopes for the best. We're working on that one.)

    1

    No mule's home address, ever

    A hub's address is never published. It's sealed hub-to-hub, so only the hubs it chooses to receive from can read it. Senders never get one; they hand parcels over at a public intake, like a Logos Circle meetup or a parcel locker.

    2

    Mules don't open the luggage

    Nobody looks inside, the same way the post office doesn't. A hub weighs and measures the box, checks one seal code, and takes off one outer sleeve. That's all.

    3

    Mules say no whenever they like

    Every hub publishes what it's willing to carry: anything, or declared categories only. A hub can refuse any parcel, and the refusal goes back to the sender.

    4

    Every handover leaves an alibi

    When the next hub confirms "received, seal intact", that receipt is the previous hub's proof that it passed the parcel on untouched. It's sealed, never public.

    5

    Only vetted mules on the route

    Routes only use hubs vouched for by a steward the sender trusts, such as a Logos Circle steward or the sender themselves. Mules are stubborn about privacy. We consider that a feature.

    Not solved yet

    Things the mules are still chewing on.

    These are written up in the repo, and they're the reason nothing real should travel yet.

    ?

    What's in the box?

    The big one. A hub can't check a sealed parcel without opening it, and opening it breaks the whole idea. We haven't found a way to keep the sender private and keep the hub safe from what it can't see. For now: no inspection, declared categories, refuse freely, and only send between people who trust each other. Ideas welcome.

    ?

    A herd of three is easy to follow

    A mixnet hides you in a crowd. With a handful of parcels, colluding hubs can link sender and recipient by timing alone. More parcels, shared batch days and decoy boxes should help; none of that is built.

    ?

    Carrier accounts want your phone number

    Lockers and couriers often need a phone number or email, which can identify a hub to the carrier. That needs per-country research.

    ?

    No way back

    A refused parcel has no return path yet, and borders need customs forms with a name on them. Domestic routes, or within the EU, for now.

    Friends first

    Got a parcel locker nearby, a few plain boxes and a kitchen scale? Read the hub guide and tell us your city. We're starting with a handful of people in the EU.

    Logos Circles
    Stewards & meetups

    Circles meet in person every month, which makes them a natural place to hand over parcels, and their stewards natural people to vouch for hubs. There are 58 active Circles in 36 countries.
